This is decision support, not a career recommendation. It ranks careers against the priorities you give it. It cannot know your circumstances, your finances, your local job market or what you would actually enjoy day to day. Use it to find candidates worth researching properly, then check the relevant professional body before you commit to anything.

How the matching works

A match score is relative compatibility, not a probability. Each career carries seven 0-10 scores. Your answers set how much you care about each of the seven, and every career is scored against the field on the axes you rated above your own average. A career you rate 84 is a closer fit to your stated priorities than one you rate 60. It is not an 84% chance of getting in, of succeeding, or of being happy.

Those seven scores are structured judgement, not measured data. They are one considered view of each career, worth a spot-check rather than treated as fact. Competition and attainability are scored separately and shown on each career report. MBTI is optional, soft-weighted and psychometrically weak, so it can nudge a ranking but never drive it.

Where the numbers come from, and their limits

Where figures are anchored, they are cited. Take-home pay uses HMRC income tax and National Insurance rules for 2026/27, and the entry routes reflect the requirements set by the relevant UK professional bodies (for example ACCA, RICS, the GMC and HCPC). Salary figures are currently indicative estimates, being migrated to cited data from the ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings. The future-proofing axis is a forecast about automation, not an observation.

This is a work in progress. The full version runs a live data pipeline that continuously refreshes these figures against official sources, so the numbers track both micro conditions (how a specific occupation is paid now) and macro conditions (where the wider market is heading). Until then, treat everything here as a considered starting point rather than the final word, and check the relevant professional body before making a decision.

CV & pivot

Paste your CV to see how it reads, how close you are to any career, and the smartest moves from where you are now, fused with your questionnaire once you've taken it.

Private by design. Your CV never leaves your browser and is never stored. PDF and .docx files are parsed on your device, only the small parser library is fetched from a CDN, never your file. This is a transparent heuristic that reads keywords and structure, not meaning, so treat it as a rough compass, not a recruiter's verdict. The meaning-aware version needs a server and is a deliberate later step.

From prototype to product

120 careers is a strong prototype library; a public build would push to hundreds, each with independently-sourced, cited salary and route data verified against the professional bodies, plus crowd-calibrated axis scores rather than one person's judgement, accounts to save results, and hosting.
